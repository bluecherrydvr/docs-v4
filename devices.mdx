---
title: Manage cameras
description: Add cameras, tune motion detection, set recording schedules, and configure PTZ.
---

Your cameras live on the **Device Management** page at `/devices`.

The table shows each camera's **Name**, **Host**, **Path**, and **Status**.
**Status** is either **Enabled** or **Disabled**.
Each row has six action buttons: **PTZ settings**, **Recording schedule**, **Motion detection**, **Check connection**, **Edit**, and **Delete**.

## Add a camera

For ONVIF cameras, discover them instead of typing stream URLs by hand (see below).
For anything else, click **Add Camera** to open the **Add New Camera** dialog.
You connect one RTSP camera at a time.

Fill in these fields:

- **Device Name**: a friendly name such as Front Door Cam.
- **Host / IP**: the camera's IP address or hostname.
- **RTSP Port**: the RTSP port (default `554`).
- **RTSP Path**: the stream path, for example `/h264/ch1/main/av_stream`.
- **Username** and **Password**: the camera's login credentials.
- **Enable Device**: leave this checked to activate the camera.

Active cameras count toward your license usage and can record and stream.
Click **Create Device** to save.

<Note>
If the camera does not appear in live view, run **Check connection** on its row, then double-check the host, port, path, and credentials.
</Note>

## Discover ONVIF cameras

Click **Discover cameras** to probe your LAN for ONVIF cameras.
Click **Find cameras** and wait a few seconds for answers.

Each answer shows its detected manufacturer (for example **Reolink**).
When detection fails, pick the maker from the list so the right defaults apply.

Check the cameras you want, optionally entering each camera's **Login** and **Password**.
Leave the login empty to try manufacturer default credentials.
Enter the real credentials when you know them: some cameras (including Reolink) briefly lock logins after repeated failures.
Click **Add selected**. The page resolves each camera's stream URLs over ONVIF and adds the camera with the correct host, port, and paths.

Cameras that are already added show an **added** badge and cannot be selected again.
If a camera fails, the dialog tells you why: **ONVIF unreachable**, **No credential worked**, or **No usable stream URL**.
Cameras without ONVIF never appear in the scan; click **Add manually** to enter one by hand.

## Check a connection

Click **Check connection** on a camera row to probe its web and MJPEG URLs.
The page reports each leg as **OK**, an HTTP status code, or **F** when unreachable.

## Edit a camera

Click **Edit** on a camera row to open the **Edit Device** dialog.
Update the connection details as needed.
Click **Update Device** to save.

## Enable or disable a camera

You enable or disable a camera through the edit form.
Click **Edit**, check or uncheck **Enable Device**, then click **Update Device**.
Disabled cameras stop recording and streaming.
There is no one-click toggle in the table yet.

## Delete a camera

Click **Delete** on a camera row.
Confirm the prompt to remove the camera permanently.

## Set up motion detection

Click **Motion detection** on a camera row to open the motion dialog.
The dialog shows a sensitivity grid over the camera frame.
Paint zones with one of six levels: **Off**, **Minimal**, **Low**, **Average**, **High**, or **Very high**.
Click or drag across cells to paint them.
Use **Fill all** to paint the whole frame with the selected level.
Use **Clear all** to turn the whole frame off.

Below the grid you can tune the detector:

- **Algorithm**: choose **Default**, **Experimental**, or **CV temporal**.
- **Downscale**: scale frames before analysis (`0.125`, `0.25`, `0.5`, or `1.0`).
- **Pre buffer (s)** and **Post buffer (s)**: seconds of video kept before and after motion.
- **Min area %** and **Max area %**: smallest and largest motion area that triggers an event.
- **Min frames** and **Max frames**: shortest and longest motion duration that triggers an event.
- **Blend ratio**: how much each frame blends into the reference image.
- **Debug snapshots**: save debug images to help you tune the settings.

Click **Save** to apply your changes.

## Set recording schedules

Click **Global schedule** to set the default schedule for all cameras.
Click **Recording schedule** on a camera row to set that camera's schedule.
Paint each hour of the week with one of five modes:

- **Off**: do not record.
- **Motion**: record when motion is detected.
- **Cont + Motion**: record continuously and flag motion.
- **Trigger**: record when an external trigger fires.
- **Continuous**: record all the time.

Click or drag across hours to paint them.
Use **Fill all** to paint the whole week with the selected mode.

A per-camera schedule only takes effect when you check **Use this schedule instead of the global schedule**.
Otherwise the camera follows the global schedule.
You can also check **Trigger ONVIF events** to forward recording events to the camera.

## Configure PTZ

PTZ setup has three parts: drivers, per-camera settings, and live control.

### Manage PTZ drivers

Click **PTZ drivers** to open the driver manager.
Each driver is a set of URL templates for an HTTP PTZ camera.
Use the `%USERNAME%`, `%PASSWORD%`, and `%ID%` placeholders in the templates.
A driver whose protocol is `onvif` speaks ONVIF instead of HTTP.

The table lists each driver's **Name**, **Protocol**, and **Port**.
Click **New driver** to add one.
Enter a **Name**, **Protocol**, and **Port**.
Leave **HTTP auth** checked to send credentials in the Authorization header.
Uncheck it to pass credentials through URL placeholders instead.
Fill in the movement commands (**Left**, **Right**, **Up**, **Down**, diagonals), **Zoom out**, **Zoom in**, **Focus in**, **Focus out**, **Stop**, **Stop zoom**, **Preset save (%ID%)**, and **Preset go (%ID%)**.
Click **Create** to save.

Deleting a driver removes PTZ from every camera that uses it.

### Assign PTZ to a camera

Click **PTZ settings** on a camera row to open the **PTZ Settings** dialog.
For an IP camera, pick a driver from the **PTZ driver** list.
Pick **No PTZ** to disable PTZ on that camera.
For an analog camera, enter the serial parameters instead: **Device path** (for example `/dev/ttyS0`), **Protocol** (for example `pelco-d`), **Addr**, **Baud**, **Bits**, **Parity**, and **Stop**.
Click **Save** to apply.

### Move the camera and use presets

Hover over a camera tile in live view to reveal the PTZ overlay.
The overlay appears only on cameras with PTZ configured.
Hold the arrow buttons to pan (**Pan left**, **Pan right**) and tilt (**Tilt up**, **Tilt down**).
Hold the plus and minus buttons to **Zoom in** and **Zoom out**.
Press **Stop** to halt movement.

To save the current position, type a name into **Save position as...** and click **Save**.
To return to a saved position, pick it from the **Preset** list and click **Go**.
Click **Delete preset** to remove the selected preset.

## Features not available yet

These camera features are planned but not built:

- Per-camera FPS and resolution tweaks.
- Video adjustments (brightness, contrast, saturation, hue).
- Re-encode settings.
- Manual motion trigger.
- A link that opens the camera's own web page.
